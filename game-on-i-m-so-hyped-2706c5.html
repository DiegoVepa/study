The mechanism: hype vs. clarity

"So Riley Brown, this AI builder guy, tweets 'game on, i'm so hyped,' no details at all. One person just replies asking what it even means. Someone else quotes a line from wherever it is, saying it's too early to talk about how they'll make money long term, and he just goes, dead on arrival. Check yourself

Why would a hyped tweet with zero details still spark both excitement and confusion?

A known builder's name alone draws attention regardless of detail. Some fans amplify the excitement while others openly ask what it even means. Hype and clarity are not the same thing.

Why do people treat "too early to share a monetization model" as a red flag instead of normal caution?

Audiences expect a credible revenue story alongside excitement, not just promises. Vague timing on monetization often signals the plan isn't settled. Skepticism grows fastest when hype outpaces specifics.

Explain in 30 seconds why one vague tweet can trigger a "dead on arrival" reaction.

The critic isn't reacting to the hype itself but to a specific dodge on business viability. "Dead on arrival" signals they've seen this pattern before and expect it to fail. The confusion from other commenters shows the pushback wasn't an isolated take.

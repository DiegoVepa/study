The US and Canada Are Quietly Working on a Tariff Deal

Why this matters this week: steel and auto tariffs could drop right before the US midterms, and critics think the timing is the real story.

The situation in 60 seconds

Reuters reports, citing sources familiar with the talks, that the US and Canada are negotiating a deal to ease US tariffs on Canadian steel and autos. This comes after broader trade talks between the two countries broke down in August 2026, straining the relationship. Officials are framing the new talks as an attempt to repair ties. But the timing lines up closely with US midterm elections on November 3, which is why some observers see this as more about domestic politics than a genuine fix to the underlying relationship.

Who wants what

United States (Trump administration) Wants: To ease tensions with a key trading partner while keeping leverage, possibly timed to midterm elections.
Leverage: Control over tariff rates and the timing of any relief.
Canada (federal government) Wants: Relief for steel and auto industries hurt by US tariffs, and a more stable trade relationship.
Leverage: Counter-tariffs and access to Canadian resources and markets the US wants.
Canadian steel and auto industries Wants: Removal of tariffs that have hurt exports and jobs.
Leverage: Political pressure on Canada's government to negotiate hard.
US midterm electorate Wants: Economic relief, lower prices, stability.
Leverage: Votes in the upcoming midterm elections.

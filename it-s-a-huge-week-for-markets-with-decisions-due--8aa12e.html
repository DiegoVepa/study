A huge week for markets

Why it matters: the Fed, the ECB and the Bank of Japan almost never decide interest rates in the same week. This week they do.

The situation in 60 seconds

This week, three of the world's most powerful central banks all announce interest rate decisions: the US Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank, and the Bank of Japan. Normally these decisions are spread out, so investors can digest one at a time. Having all three land close together is unusual, and it raises the chances of sharper moves in currencies, bonds and stocks, since markets now have to process several major signals about the direction of money at once. Reuters frames it as a pivotal stretch for global markets, with investors watching each decision closely for clues about where borrowing costs and growth are headed next.

Who wants what

US Federal Reserve

Wants: Balance inflation control with supporting growth and employment.

Leverage: Sets the benchmark rate for the world's largest economy, which influences global capital flows.

European Central Bank

Wants: Manage inflation and growth across all eurozone member states at once.

Leverage: Sets rates for the euro, affecting eurozone borrowing costs and the euro's exchange rate.

Bank of Japan

Wants: Normalize policy after years of ultra-low rates without disrupting the economy.

Leverage: Controls yen interest rates, which affects global carry trades and yen-funded investments.

Investors / markets

Wants: Predictability and clarity on future borrowing costs so assets can be priced correctly.

Leverage: Can move capital rapidly in response to rate signals, amplifying market reactions.

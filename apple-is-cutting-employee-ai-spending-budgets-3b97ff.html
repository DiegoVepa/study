Apple is quietly cutting how much it lets employees spend on AI

Why this matters: Apple wants staff to use AI more, while shrinking the budget that pays for it, a sign that even the richest tech company is watching AI costs closely.

The situation in 60 seconds

Apple has been lowering the daily dollar limit employees can spend on internal AI tools, even as leadership pushes staff to use AI more in their day-to-day work. Engineers saw their daily cap cut from $300 to $200, with a further cut to $100 reportedly planned. Other, non-engineering teams could see limits fall as low as $25 a day. Commenters online suspect the real driver is that Apple pays steep enterprise or API-level pricing for this access, far more expensive than a typical consumer AI subscription, which would explain why costs are being reined in even as AI use expands.

Who wants what

Apple leadership

Wants: Cut internal AI infrastructure costs while still scaling AI adoption across the company.

Leverage: Controls employee tool budgets and negotiates enterprise AI contracts.

Apple engineers and employees

Wants: Enough AI tool access and budget to meet rising expectations for AI-assisted output.

Leverage: Are the ones expected to actually produce the increased AI-driven output.

Enterprise and API AI providers

Wants: Maximize revenue from Apple's enterprise-tier AI usage contracts.

Leverage: Set premium enterprise and API pricing that's harder for large customers to avoid.

Anthropic's IPO Roadshow, Explained

Why this matters this week: the company behind Claude just started pitching big investors before going public, this is the step right before shares exist, but they do not exist yet.

The situation in 60 seconds

On November 10, 2026, Anthropic announced it has launched the roadshow for its initial public offering. A roadshow is the stretch where company executives present to large institutional investors to drum up demand before shares actually list. Anthropic had already filed a registration statement with the SEC, and on the same day an amended filing reportedly showed a proposed price range of $70 to $78 per share for 825 million Class A shares, aiming to raise up to $64.4 billion. The company also reportedly secured $26 billion in a private placement from investors including Google and Nvidia, and has applied to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, targeting a valuation up to $1.4 trillion. None of this is final: the SEC registration statement has been filed but is not yet effective, which Anthropic's own post states directly, so no shares can legally be bought or sold yet.

Check yourself

Why does a company file with the SEC weeks before anyone can actually buy shares?

The registration statement has to be declared effective first. The roadshow happens in between: it's the pitch to investors while that review is pending.

Who benefits most from Anthropic going public right now, and what do they get from it?

Anthropic gets capital and liquidity for early investors and employees. Public investors get access to AI exposure they previously could not buy directly.

Explain an IPO roadshow to a friend in 30 seconds.

A strong answer covers: it's the pitch tour to big investors before shares list, it happens after the SEC filing but before the offering is legally effective, and its purpose is to gauge demand and help set the price.

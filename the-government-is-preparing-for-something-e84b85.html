The Government Is Preparing For Something

Why it matters this week: after strikes on Iran, the Pentagon is quietly prepping for a long crisis while governments worldwide tell people to stock up. Two things are true at once, and it's worth knowing which is which.

The situation in 60 seconds

In October 2026 the US struck three Iranian nuclear facilities. Since then, the Pentagon has visibly shifted into long-crisis mode: Project FORTRESS aims to make 200 major bases self-sufficient for 90 days, the Army ran mass burial drills, and air defenses went up near Mar-a-Lago. At the same time, 33 countries told citizens to stockpile a few days of food, water, and cash, and several news outlets described the Pentagon's plans with the identical phrase "nightmare scenario." Underneath the noise, Iran has threatened retaliation against undersea cables, data centers, and AI facilities, and tensions have continued into November with limited strikes on both sides. The honest read: fear sells and crises tend to grow government power, but the Iran escalation risk underneath all of it is real, not fake.

The gap that makes it click

Public guidance tops out at 10 days. The military is preparing for 90. That nine-times gap is the core mechanism worth noticing.

Explain it to a friend in 30 seconds

"So the US hit Iranian nuclear sites in October, and right after that the Pentagon quietly started prepping like this could drag on for months, 90-day self-sufficient bases, mass burial drills, that kind of thing. Meanwhile dozens of governments told people to stock a few days of food and cash, and a bunch of news outlets used the exact same scary phrase at the same time. There are really three separate layers here: a real military escalation risk with Iran, media and political incentives to amplify fear, and the longer pattern where governments gain emergency powers during a crisis and mostly keep them after. The Iran risk is real. The fear packaging around it is a separate thing worth questioning."

Check yourself

Why did the Pentagon start Project FORTRESS, and what does it target?

To prepare for a prolonged grid or supply failure during an extended conflict. It targets 90 days of self-sufficiency across 200 major bases, using battery storage, portable nuclear reactors, and stockpiles.

What is the "ratchet effect" and why does it make crisis-driven power grabs sticky?

Governments gain emergency powers during a crisis that often outlast the crisis itself, for example the WWII-era income tax and the Patriot Act after 9/11. The power expands fast but rarely contracts fully once the threat fades.

In 30 seconds, why does this story have three separate layers, not one?

A strong answer separates: real military escalation risk with Iran, media and political incentives to amplify fear around midterms, and the longer-term pattern of crises permanently expanding government power. Treating all three as one story is how fear and fact get mixed together.

General background may be dated: FEMA historically recommends 72-hour household kits, so the jump to 10-day guidance abroad is a notable tone shift, not a new concept. FEMA/FCC emergency alert tests are routine and scheduled well in advance. The "ratchet effect" comes from economist Robert Higgs' book Crisis and Leviathan.

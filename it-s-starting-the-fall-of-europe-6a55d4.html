It's Starting: The Fall of Europe

Why this matters this week: riots are spreading across four European countries right now, and the fight over what's "really" causing them is becoming its own political battle.

The situation in 60 seconds

In 2026, protests and riots have spread across France, Italy, Belgium, and Portugal, including supermarket looting, a burned historic Paris market, and cleared squares in Milan. Officials mostly blame underfunded schools and public services. A video by Andrei Jikh argues that's not the real story, and stacks five deeper theories: welfare systems outrunning demographics, failed immigrant integration, profit incentives for business and defense contractors, and a surveillance state expanding under cover of crisis. Some of the numbers behind this, debt levels, fertility rates, defense stock prices, are real and checkable. The story connecting them into one engineered cause is the host's interpretation, not a settled fact.

Who wants what

European welfare states (France, etc.)

Wants: Keep social spending promises without political backlash

Leverage: Taxing and borrowing power, but boxed in by a shrinking worker base and already-high debt

Immigrant communities and enclaves

Wants: Economic opportunity and community stability

Leverage: Growing demographic share, concentrated voting blocs in some regions

Big business (e.g. Mercedes-Benz)

Wants: Cheaper, more abundant labor

Leverage: Lobbying influence, framing migration as an economic necessity

Defense industry and NATO

Wants: Higher, sustained defense budgets

Leverage: Raised NATO spending target to 5% of GDP by 2035, stock gains for firms like Rheinmetall

Nationalist / restrictionist governments (Denmark, Italy)

Wants: Mandatory integration, limits on foreign-born population share in institutions

Leverage: Legislative tools like Denmark's Ghetto Law and Italy's 2026 classroom caps

Surveillance / security-state advocates

Wants: Expanded monitoring powers

Leverage: Crisis and unrest used to justify tools like France's AI surveillance rollout

How we got here

  • 1950s-60sWest Germany recruits "guest workers" to fill labor shortages, an early template for migration-for-labor policy.
  • 2000UN publishes its "Replacement Migration" report, examining migration as a response to aging, shrinking populations.
  • 2015Germany's asylum seeker surge. Mercedes-Benz's CEO publicly calls it a potential economic opportunity.
  • 2017UAE Foreign Minister publicly warns that political correctness in Europe would produce more radicalization.
  • 2024France legalizes AI video surveillance for the Paris Olympics, framed as a temporary measure.
  • June 2025NATO members agree to raise defense spending targets to 5% of GDP by 2035.
  • September 2026Italy's school decree caps foreign-language-speaking students at 30% per classroom.
  • 2026 (now)Riots and unrest spread across France, Italy, Belgium, and Portugal, officially attributed to underfunded public services.

The mechanism in one picture

Pay-as-you-go welfare means today's workers fund today's retirees directly. When that ratio shrinks, the same benefits cost each remaining worker far more.

Check yourself

Why does the video say Europe's welfare model is running out of road right now specifically?

The worker-to-retiree ratio dropped from roughly 10:1 to near 2:1. Pay-as-you-go financing needs current workers to fund current retirees, and countries like France haven't balanced a budget since 1974, with debt around 119% of GDP.

What's the difference between the "failed integration" theory and the "intentional chaos for profit" theory?

Failed integration blames policy choices: tolerance over assimilation, resulting enclaves, weak work pressure under welfare. Intentional chaos blames deliberate incentives, cheap labor, new voters, and surveillance expansion that benefit business and political elites, framing it as design rather than accidental policy failure.

Explain in 30 seconds why the video says the riots aren't really about "leaky ceilings."

The official story points to underfunded schools, but the video argues deeper pressures, debt, demographics, failed integration, business incentives, and surveillance expansion, are the real fuel. This is the video's interpretation, not a proven fact.

Revolut cut AI coding costs about 10x by self-hosting a Chinese model

Why this matters: a major fintech just made a self-hosted open model its default over Claude Code, not a side experiment. That's a signal worth understanding.

The situation in 60 seconds

Revolut, the UK fintech, says it stopped defaulting to Anthropic's Claude Code for AI-assisted coding and switched to Z.ai's GLM, a Chinese open-weight model, run on Nvidia GPUs it owns rather than through a paid API. The reported result: AI coding costs dropped roughly 10x, down to about $0.30 per million tokens. The claim traces to a report from The Register, and a top comment on the viral post asks the obvious question: does that $0.30 figure actually include the cost of the GPUs themselves, or just the inference run on hardware Revolut already bought?

Explain it to a friend in 30 seconds

"Revolut stopped paying Anthropic per token for Claude Code and started running its own open-source model, GLM, on GPUs it owns. They say that cut their AI coding costs by about 10x, down to around $0.30 per million tokens. But that number is just inference cost. It probably doesn't count the GPUs, the power, or the engineers needed to keep it running, so the real savings might be smaller once you add those back in."

Check yourself

Why would a fintech like Revolut want to control its own AI model and GPUs instead of using Claude Code?

Cost control, data control, and not depending on a third party's pricing or availability.

Why does the $0.30 per million tokens figure get challenged in the comments?

Because it may only count inference cost, not the GPU hardware, power, or engineering needed to run it.

The cost savings claim (~10x), owning the infrastructure (Nvidia GPUs in-house), using an open model (GLM) instead of a paid API, and the caveat that the headline cost may not include hardware or operational costs.

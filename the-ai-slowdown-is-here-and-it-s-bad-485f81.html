The AI Slowdown Is Here, and It's Bad

Why this matters this week: AI stocks just had their worst drop since the 2020 crash, and Anthropic's own IPO price tag is shrinking in real time.

The situation in 60 seconds

AI-linked stocks suffered one of their sharpest selloffs since the 2020 COVID crash. Around the same time, reporting says Anthropic's IPO valuation talk has fallen toward roughly 1.3 trillion dollars, down from numbers closer to 2 trillion just weeks earlier. Underneath the panic are real costs: top-end GPUs run 30,000 to 40,000 dollars each, and labs reportedly still spend most of their compute training new models rather than serving the people paying for them. That is a big part of why Claude just added pricier 100 and 200 dollar monthly tiers. A run of security incidents, including a reported 6.3 billion dollar Binance hack and hacked supermarket fridges in Europe, adds to a sense that fast-growing, tightly connected tech systems are fragile in ways that are easy to overlook when everyone is focused on the next model release.

Who wants what

Frontier AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic)

Wants: Keep scaling model capability and revenue to justify their valuations.

Leverage: They control frontier model capability and the enterprise and API relationships built on top of it.

Investors and potential IPO backers

Wants: Confidence that AI revenue will catch up to valuations before committing more capital.

Leverage: They can withhold funding or demand lower valuations, which is exactly what seems to be happening to Anthropic's IPO talk.

Subscribers and businesses using AI tools

Wants: Capable AI at a stable, affordable price.

Leverage: They can switch providers or cut usage if prices climb too far.

Hardware suppliers (Nvidia)

Wants: Sell compute hardware at high margins while demand outstrips supply.

Leverage: They control the GPU supply and pricing power on chips like the H200.

Politicians and regulators

Wants: Influence over data center buildout, energy use, and permitting.

Leverage: They can block or slow infrastructure projects through permitting and policy, which is part of why the US midterms are being watched closely here.

What triggered the recent AI stock selloff, and why did it revive bubble talk?

The Nasdaq and S&P saw their sharpest drop since the 2020 COVID crash, with the Nasdaq Composite down 5.5% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 12.2% on November 5, 2026. Reporting that Anthropic's IPO valuation talk fell toward 1.1 to 1.3 trillion dollars amplified fears that AI valuations had gotten overheated.

Why are AI subscription prices climbing, according to the compute cost breakdown?

GPUs like the H200 cost 30,000 to 40,000 dollars each, and labs like Anthropic reportedly still spend most of their compute, around 70 percent, on training rather than serving existing users. That pushes providers to raise prices, which is part of why Claude added its 100 and 200 dollar monthly tiers.

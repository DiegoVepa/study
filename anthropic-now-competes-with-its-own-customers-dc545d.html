Anthropic now competes with its own customers

Why this matters this week: the AI company selling you an API has started admitting it might build the product you were planning to build with it.

The situation in 60 seconds

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has pointed to a $30 trillion opportunity sitting in front of the company. Bloomberg Opinion columnist Parmy Olson argues one way to actually reach a number that big is to stop just selling API access and start pushing deeper into high-value regulated industries: healthcare, law, finance and cybersecurity. The catch is that Anthropic has openly acknowledged this could put it in direct competition with some of the same businesses that currently pay to use its models. That tension, grow by going up the stack versus stay neutral infrastructure for your own customers, is the real story behind the growth headlines.

Who wants what

Anthropic / Dario Amodei

Wants: Capture a larger share of the $30 trillion opportunity by building directly in high-value regulated verticals.

Leverage: Owns the underlying foundation model (Claude), enterprise relationships, and capital to build vertical products.

Anthropic's enterprise / API customers

Wants: Build and defend their own products in healthcare, law, finance and cybersecurity using Claude.

Leverage: Domain expertise and existing customer relationships, but limited power if their platform provider enters the same space.

Incumbents in healthcare, law, finance, cybersecurity

Wants: Protect existing market share from AI-native entrants.

Leverage: Regulatory relationships, proprietary data, licensing and compliance barriers.

Bloomberg Opinion / Parmy Olson

Wants: Analyze and surface the tension in Anthropic's strategy for a business audience.

Leverage: Media platform and access to Anthropic's public statements.

The mechanism, visualized

Explain it to a friend in 30 seconds

"So Anthropic, the company behind Claude, basically admitted something most infrastructure companies never say out loud: they might start competing with the same businesses that pay to use their AI model. Dario Amodei has talked about a $30 trillion opportunity, and one way to actually get there is to stop just renting out the model and start building finished products in healthcare, law, finance, and cybersecurity. Problem is, those are exactly the industries where their own customers are already building. It is like your landlord deciding to open a competing store right next to yours."

Check yourself

Why would Anthropic risk competing with the same companies that pay to use its models?

Because the biggest margins sit in finished products for regulated industries, not just API access, so capturing that value directly is worth the customer friction.

Why do healthcare, law, finance and cybersecurity stand out as Anthropic's expansion targets?

They are high-value, high-complexity industries willing to pay a premium for AI that handles specialized, regulated work.

Why is it notable that Anthropic admitted it might compete with its own customers?

Most infrastructure providers claim to stay neutral. Anthropic openly said it won't necessarily stay that way, which is a real platform risk for any company building a product on top of Claude's API.

Background: foundation model companies sell API access to businesses that build specialized products on top of it. As these companies grow, they face a choice: stay neutral infrastructure, or move up the stack into finished products in the same verticals their customers serve. The latter earns more per customer but risks undercutting the very businesses built on the platform.

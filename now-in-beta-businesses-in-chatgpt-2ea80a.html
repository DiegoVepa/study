Businesses in ChatGPT, now in beta

ChatGPT just opened a second front door for businesses, right inside your conversation.

The situation in 60 seconds

OpenAI launched a beta called Businesses in ChatGPT. Companies can now claim a profile in a new "Business Hub," manage their own information, and see how people talk about them inside ChatGPT. Eligible businesses can go further and build a "Business Agent," a dedicated AI that answers customer questions and helps people compare products, using data the business itself provides. That agent's answers show up in a conversation clearly labeled apart from ChatGPT's own independent answers, so the two don't get mixed up. For now this is limited to ecommerce and online service businesses in the US, though claiming a basic profile is open anywhere ChatGPT is available.

Who wants what

OpenAI

Wants ChatGPT to become a discovery and commerce layer where businesses engage customers directly.

Leverage: a massive existing ChatGPT user base, plus control over how businesses are represented inside conversations.

Participating businesses

Want control over how they're described in ChatGPT and a new channel to reach and convert customers.

Leverage: their own product data, pricing feeds, and brand information fed into the Business Agent.

ChatGPT users and customers

Want accurate answers when comparing products or researching a business, without marketing dressed up as neutral information.

Leverage: trust in ChatGPT's labeling to tell independent answers apart from business-provided ones.

Competing platforms (Google, Amazon, marketplaces)

Want to stay the default place people discover and compare businesses and products.

Leverage: existing ad ecosystems, search dominance, and established merchant relationships.

How it actually works

Explain it to a friend in 30 seconds

"ChatGPT just got something like a business directory, but AI-native. Companies can claim a verified profile, manage their info, and see how people mention them in chats. Some of them can also build a Business Agent, an AI trained on their own product data, that answers customer questions and helps people compare products. The key part is that this agent's answers are kept in a separate, clearly labeled conversation, so you can always tell the difference between ChatGPT's own neutral take and a business pitching for itself. Right now it's only for ecommerce and online service businesses in the US, but it's basically a Google Business Profile for the AI era."

Check yourself

What can a business now do inside OpenAI's new Business Hub beta, and why would they want that level of control?

They can claim their business, manage their info, view engagement insights, and build a labeled Business Agent that answers customer questions using their own data. This gives them control over how ChatGPT represents them instead of leaving it to guesswork.

Why does OpenAI keep Business Agent answers in a separate, clearly labeled conversation instead of blending them into ChatGPT's main answers?

To protect trust: users can tell when they're getting a business's own curated pitch versus ChatGPT's independent, unaffiliated answer, which avoids confusion about which response is neutral and which is self-interested.

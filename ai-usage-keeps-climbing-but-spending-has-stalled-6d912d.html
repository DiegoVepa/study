AI usage is climbing. AI spending isn't keeping up.

Why it matters this week: the gap between usage and spend is starting to poke at the growth story investors have been pricing into AI stocks.

The situation in 60 seconds

More people and companies are using AI tools than ever. But the total money changing hands for that usage isn't rising at the same rate. Vendors keep cutting prices to stay competitive, and the biggest-spending companies are tightening their budgets after an initial rush of pilots. Put together, you get more usage without a matching rise in revenue, which is the opposite of the "hypergrowth" story that's been baked into AI valuations.

Who wants what

AI vendors and providers

Want: usage growth to turn into revenue growth

Leverage: better models, deeper enterprise integration, bundling products together

Biggest enterprise AI spenders

Want: the same or better AI output for less money

Leverage: buying power at scale, ability to switch vendors, internal budget scrutiny

Investors in AI companies

Want: proof that usage keeps converting into sustained revenue growth

Leverage: where they put capital next, and what valuations they're willing to pay

The mechanism, visualized

Explain it to a friend in 30 seconds

"AI usage keeps going up. But the money being spent on it isn't growing nearly as fast. Two reasons: vendors keep cutting prices to compete, and the companies spending the most are now watching their AI budgets more carefully. So more people are using it, but the dollars aren't scaling with that usage. That matters because a lot of investor optimism around AI assumes spending will keep climbing right alongside usage. If that link breaks, it's a real problem for how these companies are being valued."

Check yourself

Who are the biggest AI spenders here, and why would they be cutting costs right now instead of spending more?

The biggest spenders are likely large enterprises that scaled up AI pilots quickly. Cost controls tend to kick in once the novelty fades and finance teams start scrutinizing actual return on investment. Vendor price cuts also mean these companies need to spend less to get the same amount of usage.

Why does rising usage paired with flat spending worry investors specifically, rather than being good news?

Investors have priced in AI revenue scaling along with usage. If spending stays flat while usage climbs, revenue per user or per task is shrinking. That breaks the hypergrowth math that current valuations are built on.

Explain to a friend in 30 seconds how AI usage can go up while AI spending stays flat.

Vendors are cutting prices per use, and companies are using AI more efficiently or consolidating onto cheaper tools. The result is more total usage happening without more total dollars changing hands.

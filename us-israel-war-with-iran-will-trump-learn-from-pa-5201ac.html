US-Israel war with Iran: will Trump learn from past wars?

The ceasefire from April is fraying, Iran just got referred to the UN Security Council, and Washington is reportedly weighing new strikes. The old question is back: who planned for the week after?

The situation in 60 seconds

The US and Israel took military action against Iran's nuclear and missile programs, but unlike the 2003 Iraq War there was no long public buildup or single clear case. The justification shifted quickly between enrichment levels, missile threats, and preemption arguments. On 28 February 2026 this escalated into open war, including the killing of Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, with strikes aimed at both weapons programs and regime change. A conditional ceasefire followed on 8 April 2026, but it has not settled anything. As of 8 October 2026, Iran's nuclear chief still refuses to abandon enrichment, and the IAEA has referred Iran to the UN Security Council for blocking inspections. The Trump administration says it will not repeat Iraq-style nation-building, but analysts point out there is still no visible plan for what Iran looks like after the strikes, the same gap that turned past interventions into forever wars.

Who wants what

Trump administration (US)

Wants to stop Iran's nuclear and missile programs without a prolonged ground war or nation-building commitment.

Leverage: airpower, intelligence, ability to strike preemptively

Israel

Wants to eliminate what it sees as an existential nuclear threat from Iran.

Leverage: direct military capability and willingness to strike first

Iran

Wants to preserve its nuclear and missile programs and ensure regime survival.

Leverage: enrichment reported near bomb-grade levels, regional proxy networks

US public and Congress

Wants to avoid another forever war after Iraq and Afghanistan fatigue.

Leverage: political pressure, approval ratings tied to outcomes

How we got here

  • 2001US intervenes in Afghanistan after 9/11 to remove the Taliban and Al-Qaeda.
  • 2003US invades Iraq after a year-long public case built on WMD claims and UN resolutions.
  • 2011US and NATO use airpower in Libya to stop Gaddafi, who falls soon after; no ground troops are sent.
  • 2013 onwardObama backs away from his "red line" on Syrian chemical weapons; Russia steps in to back Assad.
  • 2021US withdraws from Afghanistan after 20 years; the Taliban retakes control.
  • 28 Feb 2026US and Israel escalate strikes on Iran's nuclear and missile programs, aiming at regime change; Khamenei and other officials are killed.
  • 8 Apr 2026A conditional ceasefire is declared, but disputes continue and diplomacy stalls.
  • 8 Oct 2026Iran refuses to drop enrichment; the IAEA refers Iran to the UN Security Council over inspection access, with Russia and China opposed to action.

Check yourself

Why does the case for action against Iran feel different from Iraq in 2003?

Iraq had a long public buildup and a specific WMD-based case. Iran's justifications shifted quickly between different threats (enrichment, missiles, preemption) without one clear sustained case.

What do Afghanistan, Libya, and Syria each show about winning a fight versus winning the peace?

Afghanistan: fast military win, no political plan, became a 20-year war. Libya: air power toppled Gaddafi with no ground plan, leading to ongoing civil war. Syria: no US intervention, and Russia filled the vacuum instead.

Why is "mission creep" the real danger in the Iran situation?

Strikes started narrow, aimed at stopping a weapons program. The administration says it won't do nation-building, but history shows narrow strikes can expand into regime change with no plan for what comes after. That gap between winning militarily and governing afterward is what turned past interventions into forever wars.

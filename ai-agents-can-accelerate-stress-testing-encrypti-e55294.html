AI Agents Just Stress-Tested Old Encryption

Why this matters this week: Google DeepMind says human-guided AI agents found two real weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA, a test case for whether AI can speed up security research itself.

The situation in 60 seconds

Google DeepMind announced that its AI models, directed by human researchers, uncovered two notable weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA encryption. That key size is a legacy standard the security industry has already been retiring for over a decade, so this is not a live threat to the encryption protecting your bank or your browser today. DeepMind is framing the news differently: it is presenting this as proof that AI agents, with humans still steering the process, can speed up the slow, grinding work of stress-testing cryptographic systems. The real story is about the method, not about a sudden crack in the internet's locks.

Who wants what

Google DeepMind

Wants: to show AI agents can meaningfully contribute to scientific and security research.

Leverage: advanced AI models paired with in-house research expertise.

Cryptography and security researchers

Wants: proof that AI genuinely speeds up vulnerability discovery, and confidence the findings are reliable.

Leverage: the domain expertise needed to guide and verify what the AI finds.

Organizations still on older RSA

Wants: clarity on whether their systems are at risk and how fast they need to migrate.

Leverage: control over their own upgrade timelines and budgets.

AI safety and dual-use critics

Wants: scrutiny over whether AI-accelerated vulnerability research could later be pointed at offensive uses.

Leverage: public and regulatory pressure on how this kind of research gets disclosed and used.

How we got here

  • 1977
    RSA encryption is introduced, becoming a foundational public-key cryptosystem.
  • 1990s to 2000s
    1024-bit RSA becomes a widely used key size across many systems.
  • Early to mid 2010s
    Standards bodies start urging retirement of 1024-bit RSA in favor of 2048-bit or higher, as computing power and factoring techniques improve.
  • 2026-11-06
    Google DeepMind publicly shares research showing AI agents, guided by humans, found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA.

Explain it to a friend in 30 seconds

"Google DeepMind had AI agents, guided by human researchers, dig into old 1024-bit RSA encryption and they found two real weaknesses in it. Headlines will make it sound like AI broke encryption, but 1024-bit RSA has been considered outdated for over ten years. Everything using real security today runs on 2048-bit or higher, which wasn't touched. The actual point they're making is that AI can speed up the slow work of security research, with humans still steering it."

Check yourself

Why use AI agents instead of relying only on human cryptographers?

To test whether AI can speed up and scale the slow, grinding process of finding weaknesses in cryptosystems.

What role did humans still play, and why does that matter?

Humans guided the AI models throughout. That means this was assisted research, not AI working fully on its own.

What is the one detail that keeps this from being a crisis?

The target was 1024-bit RSA, a key size the industry already considers outdated. Modern systems run on 2048-bit or higher, which this research did not touch.

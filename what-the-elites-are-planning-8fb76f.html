What The Elites Are Planning

Why this matters this week: ESG funds are still bleeding money, central bank digital currencies are moving from theory to pilots, and the 2030 deadline these plans are built around is now four years away.

The situation in 60 seconds

In 1992, 178 governments signed a real, public, 350-page UN document called Agenda 21, a non-binding blueprint for sustainable development covering land use and resources. In 2015 it was updated into Agenda 2030 and its 17 Sustainable Development Goals. Separately, the World Economic Forum has run messaging about a 2030 where "you own nothing," Klaus Schwab launched the Great Reset in 2020, and asset managers like BlackRock talk openly about using ESG scoring and shareholder votes to push corporate behavior. A video by Andrei Jikh, drawing on commentator G. Edward Griffin, argues these are not separate, mostly voluntary initiatives but one coordinated plan by global elites to centralize control over property, food, and money. Mainstream analysts describe them instead as unrelated frameworks, marketing language, and investment strategy that got woven into a single story.

Who wants what

United Nations

Wants: Coordinate global sustainability and development policy among member states.

Leverage: Treaties and frameworks like Agenda 21 and the SDGs, non-binding but influential on national and local policy.

World Economic Forum / Klaus Schwab

Wants: Position itself as the convener of government and corporate elites around "stakeholder capitalism."

Leverage: The annual Davos summit and published reports and predictions that set narrative framing.

BlackRock / Larry Fink

Wants: Manage risk and returns for clients while publicly aligning with ESG trends.

Leverage: Over $10 trillion in assets under management and voting power at shareholder meetings.

European Central Bank / Christine Lagarde

Wants: Modernize eurozone settlement infrastructure with digital and tokenized assets.

Leverage: Central bank authority over euro monetary policy and digital currency pilots.

Critics (G. Edward Griffin, Andrei Jikh, and similar commentators)

Wants: Warn the public that these initiatives add up to a coordinated loss of ownership and autonomy.

Leverage: Media reach on YouTube and in books, connecting disparate quotes and documents into one narrative.

How we got here

  • 1976A UN Habitat conference states that public control of land use is needed to prevent wealth concentration.
  • 1992178 governments sign Agenda 21 at the Rio Earth Summit; a related US House resolution follows.
  • 1993President Clinton creates the President's Council on Sustainable Development by executive order.
  • 2015193 UN member states adopt Agenda 2030 and its 17 Sustainable Development Goals.
  • 2016The WEF publishes "8 predictions for 2030," including "you'll own nothing and be happy."
  • 2020Klaus Schwab launches the Great Reset, calling the pandemic a window to reshape the global economy.
  • 2020sCentral banks including the ECB advance digital currency pilots; asset managers expand ESG scoring, then face political pushback.

Contested

ICLEI and local sustainability programs are real, but the intent to "bypass" democratic process is disputed and has been a claim from Agenda 21 critics since the 1990s.

Contested

Fertility decline is a well documented demographic trend with established economic and social causes. Linking it to a deliberate elite plan is an inference, not something demonstrated.

That figure comes from the 1992 document's own estimate for voluntary development aid and programs, not a confirmed covert transfer mechanism.

Check yourself

What is Agenda 21/2030, and who actually signed it?

A non-binding UN sustainability framework signed by 178 governments in 1992, updated into 17 Sustainable Development Goals adopted by 193 members in 2015. It covers development, land use, and resource policy, and carries no legal enforcement mechanism.

Name two things this video treats as one plan that are actually separate and independent.

Any two of: the WEF's 2016 prediction video (later removed), Larry Fink's ESG/BlackRock comments, Lagarde's digital euro project, or Schwab's Great Reset. Each is real but was launched independently by a different organization for its own stated purpose.

What detail does the video's framing leave out about the WEF's "you'll own nothing" video?

The WEF pulled the video down itself in 2016 after public backlash that it misrepresented a possible future scenario as a stated goal. Leaving that out makes the quote sound like an ongoing, unapologetic plan rather than a retracted piece of messaging.
